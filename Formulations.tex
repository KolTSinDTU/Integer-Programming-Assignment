\section*{Question 10}

\paragraph{Greedy principle.}
Starting from an empty selection, we repeatedly add the route with the
lowest \emph{effective cost per newly covered location},
\[
r_j = \frac{c_j + \sum_{i \in S_j \cap U} \rho_i}{|S_j \setminus U|},
\]
where $U$ is the set of locations already covered. The numerator is the route
cost plus the over-coverage penalties the route would add, and the
denominator is the number of uncovered locations it covers. Only routes
covering at least one new location are considered, and ties are broken by
the lowest route index. The procedure stops when all ten locations are
covered, so the solution is feasible.

\paragraph{Iterations.}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clcl@{}}
\toprule
It. & Selected route & $r_j$ & Covered after iteration \\
\midrule
1 & S2 $\{1,3,4\}$  & $8/3 = 2.67$          & $\{1,3,4\}$ \\
2 & S3 $\{2,6\}$    & $7/2 = 3.50$          & $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ \\
3 & S8 $\{8,9,10\}$ & $14/3 = 4.67$         & $\{1,2,3,4,6,8,9,10\}$ \\
4 & S4 $\{3,7\}$    & $(6+1)/1 = 7$         & $\{1,\dots,4,6,\dots,10\}$ \\
5 & S1 $\{1,2,5\}$  & $(9+2+1)/1 = 12$      & $\{1,\dots,10\}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Solution.}
The greedy solution is $\{S1, S2, S3, S4, S8\}$. Locations 1, 2 and 3 are
each covered twice, and no route can be removed without leaving a location
uncovered. The objective value is
\[
\underbrace{9 + 8 + 7 + 6 + 14}_{\text{route costs} \,=\, 44}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\rho_1 + \rho_2 + \rho_3}_{2+1+1 \,=\, 4}
\;=\; 48 .
\]
